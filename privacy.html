BALL

Privacy

What BALL keeps about you, why, for how long, and how to take it back. Last updated 6 November 2026.

The short version

  • BALL is a free football quiz made by one fan. No ads, no ad trackers, nothing is sold.
  • Play as a guest and nothing about you leaves your phone.
  • Found your club and we keep your email (for signing in only), your username, your club and your game stats, so your career is there on every phone.
  • Crash reports and a few usage counts help us keep the app working. They carry a random id for your install, not your name or account.
  • Export or delete everything yourself, in Settings.

Who is responsible

Martijn Jeurissen hello@ballquiz.app

That is the controller in the sense of the GDPR: the one who decides what is kept and why, and the one to write to with any question about your data.

What we keep, and why

Each purpose below says what is kept, the legal basis for it, and how long it stays.

Your club and your career

Once you found your club: your email address (for signing in only; nobody else sees it), your username, your club's design (name, crest, kit, scarf, a motto from our list), the club and national team you support and the country you live in (if you picked them), whether you told us you are 16 or older, and your game stats: matches, rating, XP, titles, trophies, leagues and your answers to questions. No real name, no birth date, no location, no contacts, no photos.

Basis: the agreement between us (the terms): this is what keeping your club means. Kept: until you delete your account.
Your game on every phone

Your game progress and settings, so they follow you to every phone you sign in on: your streaks and bests, the games you have played and how far you got, your album and your locker, and how you like BALL set up (sound, answer style, theme, the competitions you pinned). Not the match you are in the middle of, nothing about the phone itself, and nothing while you play as a guest.

Basis: the agreement between us: this is what keeping your club on every phone means. Kept: until you delete your account.
Leagues with your friends

Which leagues you are in, your points and the weekly tables. League mates see your club name, crest, kit, scarf, @username, level and rating.

Basis: the agreement between us. Kept: while you are in the league. When you delete your account, your name, id and club are removed from every closed week; only an empty place and its points remain.
Call It, the prediction league

The score you call for each match, where you put your armband, your points, and the leagues you are in. Nobody sees what you called before kick-off; afterwards your league mates do. Everyone's calls together (how many backed each result, never who) can be shown after kick-off too. A guest who calls matches does so under an anonymous account without an email address, so the calls can count.

Basis: the agreement between us. Kept: while your account and the league live; Call It stores ids only, so deleting your account takes your calls with it.
Keeping names clean

Club names, usernames and the name on your shirt pass a word filter. A report about a name (who reported, which name, the reason, a note) and an appeal you send ("ask for a second look") are kept for our weekly look.

Basis: our legitimate interest in a game you can play with your friends without abuse. Kept: until we have had a look and cleared them; they go with your account.
Crash reports and usage events

When the app breaks: the error message, the place in our code, a few lines of the stack, the build, the screen you were on and a coarse browser label like "Chrome 141 · Android". A few events as counts: first open, first answer, a match done, a Daily done, a share, a club founded. Each carries a random id made on your phone the first time it sends anything (22 characters). That id is not your account and is never joined to it, so none of this can be traced back to your name, club or email. It does tie one install's events together, so we treat them as personal data. No IP address is written down, no free text you typed, no answers to questions.

Basis: our legitimate interest in an app that works, and in seeing where new players give up. Kept: 180 days, then deleted automatically.
Question reports

When you tap "Something wrong with this question?": the question and our answer, the reason you picked and a note of up to 300 characters, plus your account id if you are signed in, so a reply is possible.

Basis: our legitimate interest in questions that are right. Kept: until the report is handled, then one more year; your account id is removed the moment you delete your account.
The news email

Only if you ticked "Tell me about new modes": we send an email now and then about what is new in BALL.

Basis: your consent; untick it in Settings any time. Kept: while it is ticked.
Reminders (if you turn them on)

If you switch on reminders, your browser gives us a push address and keys, and we keep those with your timezone and which reminders you want (your streak at risk in the evening, your Call It card about to lock). One a day at most. The message travels through your browser maker's push service (Google, Mozilla or Apple, depending on your browser), encrypted so only your phone can read it.

Basis: your consent; switch them off in Settings or in your browser's site settings. Kept: until you switch them off or delete your account.
Guests

Everything you do as a guest is saved on your phone only (your browser's local storage) and is never sent to us. The one exception is anything you choose to send yourself: a question report, or your Call It calls as a guest, under an anonymous account without an email address.

Basis: none needed, nothing leaves the phone. Kept: until you clear the app's data.
DART and RACKET

DART and RACKET, the darts and tennis games you reach from BALL, keep your name, your scores and your settings on your phone only. None of it is sent to us.

Basis: none needed, nothing leaves the phone. Kept: until you clear the app's data.

Signing in

You sign in with a code (or a link) we email you, sent for our sign-in provider Supabase by our email service Resend, or with Google. If you sign in with Google, we receive your email address and nothing else from your Google account; the sign-in itself is handled by Google under Google's privacy policy. There are no passwords in BALL.

Who sees what

League mates see your club name, crest, kit, scarf, @username, level and rating. Under 16, others see you as "Player NNNN" and your club stays private. Reports are anonymous to the person reported. Fanbase Battle has a public table that anyone can read, with totals per club only: passes, medals and how many fans a club has. Your passes count towards those totals once you have founded a club, and they come off if you delete your account; no name, username or account is ever on it. Nobody sees your email address but us, our sign-in provider and the service that sends its emails.

Where it lives, and who helps us

  • Supabase (database and sign-in), in Ireland (AWS eu-west-1, in the EU). The small programs that delete an account and that send the reminders (Edge Functions) also run on Supabase. Supabase processes your data on our behalf and under our instructions.
  • Resend sends the sign-in emails from ballquiz.app, from its servers in Ireland (eu-west-1, in the EU). It receives your email address and the email it sends you, and nothing else; it processes them on our behalf and under our instructions.
  • GitHub Pages hosts the app's pages. Like any web host, GitHub's servers keep ordinary access logs (your IP address, the time, the page asked for) under GitHub's privacy statement; we never see them.
  • Google, only if you choose to sign in with Google (see above).
  • API-Football sends us fixtures and results for Call It and the match centre. Nothing about you goes there.
  • Your browser's push service, only if you turn reminders on (see above).

Supabase is a company from Singapore and Resend one from the United States, and their staff and systems can handle your data outside the EU. That is covered by the EU's standard contractual clauses and, where it applies, the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

No analytics company, no advertising network, no data broker. We don't sell your data and never will.

People in our questions

BALL's questions are about real people in sport: players, managers and referees. For those questions we keep their names and public facts about their careers, like nationality, year of birth, position, clubs, caps, goals and honours. These come from public sources such as Wikipedia, Wikidata and published results. We don't use their photos.

Basis: our legitimate interest in a sports quiz, which can't exist without the game's own history. We can't reach everyone in our questions directly, so this page is how we tell them. If you are one of them, write to hello@ballquiz.app: you can ask what we hold, have it corrected, or object to it, and we answer within a month. Kept: while the question is in the game.

Cookies and your phone

BALL sets no cookies. It uses your browser's local storage for your game on this phone: your progress, settings and club, your sign-in session, the random install id, and the queue of things waiting to sync. It also keeps an offline copy of itself so it opens without a signal. Clearing the site's data in your browser wipes all of it from that phone.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you may ask to see what we hold about you (access), have it corrected (rectification), have it deleted (erasure), take it with you (portability), object to what we do on the basis of our legitimate interest (objection), and withdraw any consent you gave, at any time.

  • See and take it: Settings → Export my data downloads everything as one file, your Call It calls and leagues included.
  • Correct it: rename your club or change your username in Settings.
  • Delete it: Settings → Delete my account removes your profile, club, matches, leagues, calls and reports from the server. What is on your phone stays until you clear it.
  • Withdraw consent: untick "Tell me about new modes" or switch reminders off, in Settings.
  • Anything else: write to hello@ballquiz.app. We answer within a month.

If you think we handle your data wrongly, you can complain to the Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, or to the authority in the EU country where you live.

Children

In the Netherlands you can agree to an online service's use of your data from the age of 16. When you found your club we ask once whether you are 16 or older. Under 16, everything still works and you keep your club and all you earn, but friends, leagues and a public username are off, and so are reminders and the news email, and other players see you only as "Player NNNN". We never ask for a birth date. A parent who wants a child's account gone can write to us.
